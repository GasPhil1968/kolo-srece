# NIGHT LATCH — Game Design Bible

> *"There's a moment, just before you open your eyes, when you're nobody in particular.
> Most mornings it passes."*

A noir point-and-click detective adventure in eight chapters. Brackwater, a rain-soaked
harbor city, October 1951. Private investigator **Thomas Rook** keeps waking up in locked
rooms with days of his life missing. Every escape leads deeper into the disappearance of a
stenographer named **Nora Marsh** — and into what was done to Rook's own mind two years ago.

Target length: ~120 minutes for a first-time player (8 chapters × 12–18 min).

---

## 1. The Truth (what really happened)

This section is the fixed ground truth. Every clue in the game is derived from it.

### October 21, 1949 — the *Meridian*
* The freighter **SS Meridian** (owned through the shell company **Gullwing Holdings** by
  Harbor Commissioner **Augustus Crane**) carried smuggled morphine under a false manifest.
  Fourteen dockworkers on the night loading shift discovered it and agreed to go to the police.
* Crane ordered the ship burned for the insurance and to destroy the cargo record.
  **Det. Lt. Frank Delaney**, already on Crane's payroll, drove to Pier 9 at **22:25**,
  padlocked the forward hold hatch with a brass Gullwing padlock at about **22:40**
  (watchman Gus Tolliver heard the hammering of the hasp), and left. The fire was set in
  the galley and reported at **23:15**. All fourteen men in the hold died.
* One of them was **Walter Marsh**, Nora's father. Walter had gone aboard at **22:05** at the
  request of Detective Sergeant **Thomas Rook**, who had asked him to look at the cargo.
  The harbor gate log records Walter's sign-in with the remark *"for Sgt. Rook"*.
* Delaney wrote the official report: galley fire, hatch "found open", arrival on scene
  23:30. He dismissed the watchman's statement as drunken.

### November 1949 — the first erasure
* Rook refused to let the case go. Delaney slipped him **Lethe** — a refined scopolamine
  compound developed by **Dr. Lucian Vey** at **Halcyon House**, a private "rest hospital"
  on Gull Hill funded by Crane — and then had him admitted to Halcyon House for
  "acute alcoholic amnesia" (2–9 Nov 1949, Room 8). Four "L-series" sessions suppressed his
  memories of the Meridian inquiry, including his request to Walter.
* Rook was suspended for "erratic conduct", resigned in 1950 and became a private
  investigator at 14 Marrow Street. He believes he drank himself off the force.
* Three surviving witnesses (dockhands Doyle, Kerrigan, Fisk) were also "treated" and
  discharged without memories. Reporter **Wendell Pike** began investigating Halcyon House
  in August 1951 and was admitted as "Patient 14".

### October 1951 — the present
* **Oct 3**: Nora Marsh, a stenographer at the Harbor Commission, takes verbatim shorthand
  minutes of a secret night session: Crane, Vey and Delaney discuss the last Lethe
  consignment aboard the **MV Calypso** (sailing Oct 21, 02:00, to be "lost at sea" for the
  insurance like the Meridian), the treatment of remaining witnesses, and Rook
  ("Rook is your department, Lieutenant Delaney. You handled him once.").
  Nora types a private three-page transcript and keeps her steno pad.
* **Oct 8**: Nora brings the transcript to Rook. Reading it, Rook realizes his "drinking"
  was chemical. He plans for the worst:
  - He splits the three transcript pages: **page 1** frozen inside a cod in the Pier 9
    cold store (row B, fish 3), **page 2** given to pianist **Deacon Boone**, **page 3**
    kept by Nora (hidden in her father's hollow-handled pocket knife).
  - He hides Nora with Sister Agnes at **St. Brigid's Mission**, disguised as a volunteer —
    and arranges *not to know* the details himself.
  - He tells his secretary Mabel: if he ever returns not knowing his own name, tell him only
    *"Ask the piano."* He tells Deacon: *"If I come back asking for her, it means they've got
    a leash on me. Lock me up."*
  - He writes himself a note and tucks it in his hatband.
* **Oct 9**: Rook meets Delaney at the Blue Lantern, intending to sound him out. Bartender
  **Sal Moretti**, coerced by Delaney (who holds his brother's gambling debts), puts Lethe in
  Rook's drink. Rook is taken to Halcyon House, where Vey gives him a massive dose that wipes
  six days. Vey's theory: *"The body remembers the routes the mind has forgotten. Let him
  retrace them. He will lead us to her."*
* **Oct 15**: Rook is dumped, locked in, at the Hotel Sorrento. Vey's head nurse **Vivian
  Hale** poses as Nora's sister "Evelyn Marsh" to hire him and track his progress.

### Why Rook keeps waking up in locked rooms
| Ch | Room | Who locked it | Why |
|----|------|---------------|-----|
| 1 | Room 9, Hotel Sorrento | Delaney & orderly Kroll | Dumped after the six-day wipe; locked "because he sleepwalks" |
| 2 | Cold store, Pier 9 | Kroll (sapped him) | Caught where page 1 was hidden; held "on ice" for re-dosing |
| 3 | Gazette darkroom | **Rook himself** | Dosed with anise-laced coffee by "Evelyn"; locked himself in before he became suggestible, hid the key behind a puzzle a drugged mind cannot solve |
| 4 | Room 8, Halcyon House | Vivian & Vey | Captured; treated again; given a post-hypnotic suggestion: *"When you find her, do call."* |
| 5 | Holding cell, 1st Precinct | Delaney | Arrested on Vivian's false complaint that he abducted Nora |
| 6 | Blue Lantern cellar | **Deacon** (a friend) | Keeping the promise Rook made him give on Oct 8 |
| 7 | Hold of the MV Calypso | Delaney's men | Rook, compelled by Vey's suggestion, phoned Halcyon from the Mission and betrayed Nora's hiding place; both are to be "lost at sea" |
| 8 | WBRK broadcast booth, City Hall | **Rook himself** | Injected by Vivian in the corridor; made it into the booth and locked it before the drug took hold, leaving notes for himself |

Nothing supernatural, no dream: scopolamine ("devil's breath", *burundanga*) really does
produce compliance and amnesia. Lethe is a fictional refinement of a real effect.

## 4. Themes and Motifs

* **Locked rooms** — the Meridian's padlocked hold is the original locked room; every
  chapter's confinement echoes it, and the last one is a room Rook chooses.
* **Notes to self** — the hatband, the notebook's back cover, grease pencil on walls, the
  photograph of a hiding place. Rook learns to trust the man he was.
* **Light** — lamplighter, safelight, red writing that vanishes in red light, matches in a
  hatband, a lantern in a smuggler's tunnel, the ON AIR lamp.
* **Clocks** — Vey's collection, the darkroom timer, the City Hall clock tower.
* **Partners** — the engraved lighter Rook carries from the first minute is the last item
  he uses.

## 5. Interface

* Native resolution **320×180**, integer-scaled with nearest neighbour (×4 = 1280×720,
  ×6 = 1920×1080). Scene viewport 320×144 (MI2 proportions), 36 px bottom panel.
* **Left click**: walk / default action (open, take, talk, use…). **Right click**: look.
* Inventory in the bottom panel; click an item to pick it up as the cursor, then click a
  hotspot, character or another item. Right-click an item to examine it.
* **Tab / Space** (or the lantern button) briefly highlights all hotspots — no pixel hunting.
* Dialogue choices appear in the bottom panel. Some conversations accept evidence
  ("Show…").
* **Notebook**: Case (objectives), Clues, People, Deductions (link two clues), Timeline.
* **Map**: travel between unlocked locations.
* **Menu**: save/load (6 slots + autosave), settings (music, SFX, text speed, pixel-perfect
  scaling, fullscreen), contextual hints (three tiers per objective), quit to title.

## 6. Art Direction

Original pixel art in the spirit of early-90s LucasArts backgrounds: hue-shifted material
ramps (cool shadows, warm highlights), ordered dithering, pooled lamplight, neon, rain and
fog. All art is generated from source code in `art/` (no third-party sprites): each room is a
composed scene of hand-specified props, materials and light sources rendered through a
ramp/luminance pipeline. Characters come from a skeletal pose renderer with hand-placed
facial pixels, supersampled and mode-downsampled so edges stay hard.

## 7. Audio Direction

Original noir jazz synthesized in `audio/`: muted trumpet, tenor sax, piano, upright bass,
brushes, vibraphone, celesta and organ for the sanatorium, strings for revelations.
Location music crossfades; ambience loops (rain, harbor, club, freezer hum, typing pool,
ship hold); footsteps per surface; mechanism, door and inventory foley; stingers for
discoveries and danger.
